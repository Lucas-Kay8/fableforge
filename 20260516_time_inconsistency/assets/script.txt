In the coastal city of Fogport, survival depended on one rule…… the lighthouse must burn every evening, without fail.

For generations, the flame was a promise. Sailors didn't need to see the rocks…… they only needed to see the light.

Then came a clever ruler. He looked at the ledgers and saw only costs. "Why burn oil…… when the seas are calm?" he asked.

"If a ship appears, we'll light it then." He decided to delay. The first night…… nothing happened. He saved oil, and felt brilliant.

Soon, the light became unpredictable. Some nights it was late…… some nights, it stayed dark. The ledger looked beautiful.

But the sailors noticed. "Fogport is no longer reliable," they whispered. One by one, the ships began to steer elsewhere.

The harbor grew silent. The ruler was confused: "We only saved a little oil. Why…… has everyone vanished?"

Because a promise is only valuable when people believe it. Once you start calculating its cost…… it is no longer a promise.

This is Time Inconsistency: what's optimal to promise today…… becomes the first thing you want to betray tomorrow.

In leadership and life, your credibility isn't built on what you say…… but on what you refuse to recalculate.
